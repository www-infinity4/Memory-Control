01 / 05 — Foundation

Classical Time: One Actor, One Arrow

In the simplest picture, time is a single arrow — past on the left, future on the right. A lone actor moves along it linearly. No other mass exists, so no gravity, no curvature, no interference. This is the Newtonian baseline: absolute, uniform, universal.

Linear Absolute Single Actor No Curvature
Actor A moves at constant velocity along a flat time axis. No other mass → spacetime is perfectly flat → t ticks identically everywhere.
Key insight: Classical time only works cleanly with a single observer in flat space. The instant you introduce a second mass, the problem becomes fundamentally different.
02 / 05 — Complexity Begins

Two Actors in 3D — Gravity Neither Planned

Add a second mass and spacetime curves around both of them — even if they never directly interact. Actor A's motion creates a gravitational ripple. Actor B sits in a different region of the grid, uninvolved in A's decision to move, yet the geometry it occupies warps because of A.

2 Masses Curved Grid Propagating Ripple Time Dilation
Watch the spacetime grid bend around each mass. When Actor A accelerates, a ripple (gravitational wave) travels outward at c — Actor B doesn't "know" yet until the wave arrives.
Why this gets complicated fast: Each mass curves space for the other. The curved path each follows changes their relative velocity → their clocks tick at different rates → their "now" slices diverge. Two actors, infinite possible configurations. Add a third and the equations have no closed-form solution.

03 / 05 — Mechanism

Gravitational Coupling: The Uninvited Effect

This is the core of "how it gets really complicated fast." Each additional actor adds a coupling term to every other actor. With N bodies, there are N(N−1)/2 coupling pairs — growing quadratically. And each coupling includes a light-travel-time delay, meaning the system is never in a single coherent "now."

N-Body Problem Light-Delay Coupling No Closed Form N≥3 Retarded Potentials
Coupling count vs. actors: 2 actors = 1 pair. 3 actors = 3 pairs. 10 actors = 45 pairs. Each pair has its own delay and phase.
Retarded potential: B's experience of A's gravity uses A's position at the retarded time — where A was when the wave left, not where A is now.
Memory relevance: If memories are stored or retrieved via field interactions (whatever the medium), the same coupling problem applies — each "stored packet" interacts with every other with a propagation delay. The brain's recall wave would arrive at different packets at different times, explaining why memory replay is compressed, non-linear, and partial.
04 / 05 — Quantum Regime

Quantum Time: Superposition, Branching & Collapse

In quantum mechanics, time itself is not an operator — it is a parameter. But quantum paths through time are all simultaneously taken (Feynman path integrals). The actor doesn't travel one route — it travels all routes at once, weighted by probability amplitude. Observation collapses this into a single classical history.

Superposition Path Integrals Wavefunction Collapse Non-local Correlations
All quantum paths from source to detector exist simultaneously (green/violet threads). At the moment of observation, one path is selected — the others never "happen." This is the quantum equivalent of the "boiling fog of future thoughts" collapsing into a single outcome.
Quantum ↔ classical boundary: Large objects decohere quickly — quantum superposition collapses into classical single-path behaviour in femtoseconds for anything brain-scale or larger, unless isolated at near-absolute-zero.
Entanglement across time: Two entangled particles share a quantum state non-locally — measuring one instantly constrains the other regardless of distance. This is a genuine non-classical correlation, but it cannot transmit information faster than c.
A

Quantum particle leaves source. Its wavefunction spreads in all directions simultaneously — it is in superposition of all possible paths to the future.

B

Two (or more) actors entangled. Their wavefunctions are correlated — A's state is not independent of B's, even across space. This is the "non-local" part that seems to defy ordinary time.

C

Observation / decoherence. Contact with the environment forces a single classical outcome. The "branching tree" of possible futures collapses to one branch — this is what we experience as "the present."

05 / 05 — The Model

Spiral Galaxy: Past & Future as Inertial Arms

A spiral galaxy's arms are not fixed structures — they are density waves, patterns left by the inertia of stars that have already passed through and stars yet to arrive. If time behaves similarly, the past arm is the compressed wake of inertia already expended, and the future arm is the probability-dense fog of trajectories being focused into existence.

Density Wave Inertial Wake Probability Fog Focused Future
Centre: the present moment. Trailing arm (blue): past — crystallised, determined events. Leading arm (violet): future — a diffuse probability field, becoming denser (more determined) as attention/energy focuses it. Stars = memory events. Density = recall strength.
Synthesis: Classical time → one arm, one actor. Add a second mass → the arms interact, coupling their wakes. Quantum time → each arm is a superposition until observed. The spiral is the full picture: inertia trailing behind, focused probability leading ahead, and the present a razor-thin rotation point between them.
Memory connection: Memories are the density-wave pattern left in the past arm — highest density where recall is strongest. End-of-life gamma surges may be a final "full rotation" where the present sweeps back through the entire past arm at once.
